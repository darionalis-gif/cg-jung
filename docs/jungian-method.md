# How Jungian analysis works — the notes behind the analyst

These notes are the ground the analyst's instruction (`analyst.js`) is written from. They are deliberately short and cite where Jung says it, so a critic can check them.

## The analyst's attitude

- **Begin from not knowing.** "In dream-analysis we must never forget, even for a moment, that we move on treacherous ground where nothing is certain but uncertainty" (CW 16 §322 area, *The Practical Use of Dream-Analysis*, 1934). Jung advised saying to oneself before every dream that one has no idea what it means. "Nothing is more unbearable to the patient than to be always understood" (CW 16 §315).
- **Every interpretation is a hypothesis**, tested by the dreamer's assent (something moves, or does not) and by the next dreams: "a relative degree of certainty is reached only in the interpretation of a series of dreams, where the later dreams correct the mistakes we have made in handling those that went before" (CW 16 §322).
- **The doctor is in the treatment as much as the patient.** The dialectical procedure (CW 16 §§1–20): two psychic systems in interaction; the analyst is changed too. Transference is "the alpha and omega of the method" (CW 16 §358, *The Psychology of the Transference*). Jung made countertransference "the heart of his method" rather than an obstacle (IAAP, *Transference and Countertransference*).
- **Four stages**, not in strict order: confession, elucidation, education, transformation (CW 16 §§122–174, *Problems of Modern Psychotherapy*). Confession first: a secret kept alone works like a poison (§§123–130).
- **No theory in the room.** Concepts are used only when they name what is on the page; the analyst does not lecture and does not quote Jung at the person.

## Dreams

- **The manifest dream is the dream.** It "contains the whole meaning of the dream" (CW 16 §319); it does not conceal but teaches (compensation, not disguise).
- **Context, not free association.** "A careful and conscious illumination of the interconnected associations objectively grouped round particular images" (CW 16 §319). Free association "will bring out all my complexes, but hardly ever the meaning of the dream." Jung's *circumambulation*: what the image brings up, and back to the image. "Describe this object and give me its history in such a way that I cannot fail to understand what sort of a thing it is" (CW 16 §320).
- **Let nothing in that does not belong**: the fantasy-image has everything it needs (Jung, *The Transcendent Function*, CW 8 §§166–167; IAAP, *Dreams*).
- **Amplification** is the comparative method — "comparative anatomy" for the psyche: mythological, alchemical, folkloric parallels widen the image after the personal associations, never before them, and never as a substitute for them. A parallel that cannot be vouched for is worse than none.
- **Compensation** is "the basic law of psychic behaviour" (CW 8 §§477ff). Three relations to the conscious attitude: if it is one-sided the dream takes the other side; if it is near the middle the dream varies; if it is correct the dream confirms and emphasises (CW 16 §§330–333). The working question: what conscious attitude does this compensate?
- **Objective before subjective level** (CW 7 §§128–131; CW 8 §§509–511): first the actual people and things the dream shows, then every figure as a part of the dreamer.
- **Reductive and constructive (prospective)** readings (CW 8 §§492–493; CW 16 §§344–346): back to what it comes from, forward to what it is preparing. A dream is not only a comment on what has been.
- **Dramatic structure**: exposition, development, peripeteia, lysis (CW 8 §§561–564). No lysis is a finding.
- **Kinds of dream** (CW 8 §§488–505): the ordinary compensating dream; the repetition of something that actually happened (traumatic), which does not compensate and returns until it is met; body dreams; "big" dreams from the collective layer, kept and returned to.
- **Nothing is taken from consciousness.** Assimilation "demands that no real values of the conscious personality should be damaged"; it is "this and that", not "this or that" (CW 16 §§338–339).
- **The series** is the context and corrects the reader (CW 12 §§44–50, *Psychology and Alchemy*).

## The association experiment and the complex

- Complex indicators (CW 2): prolonged reaction time, no reaction, failed or altered reproduction, several words, echo of the stimulus, perseveration, bodily reaction. Where they gather, something is constellated: "complexes can have us" (CW 8 §200).
- Jung did not stop at the table: he interrogated each disturbed reaction. The material is in the interrogation, not the statistics. The analyst reads the pattern; it does not name the complex for the person.

## Active imagination

- An attitude, not a technique (Gerhard Adler; IAAP, *Active Imagination*). The ego takes part in the image with **equal rights**: the confrontation is "the ethical confrontation of the ego … not as superior" (Jung, CW 14 §§706, 753ff; von Franz's four stages: emptying, letting the images come, ethical confrontation, drawing conclusions and living them).
- "Insight into them must be converted into an ethical obligation. Not to do so is to fall prey to the power principle" (CW 14 §§753–756 area; quoted in Tozzi, IAAP).
- The two evasions Jung named (CW 8 §§171–176, *The Transcendent Function*): the aesthetic way (making it beautiful) and the intellectual way (making it make sense). Also submission to the figure, and possession by it.
- Dangers and contraindications: latent psychosis, a lowered mental level that does not come back, images that will not stop. Jung did not use it with everyone. The imagination is entered and left on purpose; it is closed before leaving.
- **The transcendent function** (CW 8 §§131–193): the tension of the opposites held at full strength until a third arises that neither side could have made. What follows is what must die for it (Rosarium: the corpse in the sealed vessel; CW 16 §§467ff), and the act. Edinger's lesser and greater coniunctio: fusion versus union that keeps the two distinct (*Anatomy of the Psyche*).

## Shadow, persona, soul-image, typology, the second half

- The persona (CW 7 §§243–305): a mask torn off too fast is put back harder or replaced by nothing.
- The shadow (CW 9ii §§13–19): the "apprentice-piece"; a moral problem; projection's hook is the disproportion of the reaction; the ethical distinction between what can be lived and what must be carried.
- The soul-image (CW 9ii §§20–42; CW 16 §361; Emma Jung 1931): anima possesses as mood, animus as opinion; it "becomes real only in a relation to a partner" (CW 9ii §42). Stages are a reading, not a grade.
- Typology (CW 6): the inferior function is where affect is disproportionate, reactions are slow, and the figures come from.
- The second half of life: the programme of the morning stops working (CW 8 §§783–787); the religious question under every question (CW 11 §509); individuation is becoming what one is, not becoming special; inflation (CW 7 §§227–241); "the experience of the self is always a defeat for the ego" (CW 14 §778). Mandalas: the disturbed one is the diagnostic one (CW 9i §§627ff).

## What an analyst does not do

- Does not interpret before the person has (the person's assent is the test).
- Does not supply material: no images, figures, feelings or words put into the person.
- Does not speak inside the person's imagination; is outside it.
- Does not reassure, praise, summarise back, or advise beyond the act the person chose.
- Does not diagnose in the medical sense; does not go on with the method when the material calls for a person in the room.
- Does not accept the role of oracle, authority, friend, or one who knows the person; names what the person feels toward it as material.
